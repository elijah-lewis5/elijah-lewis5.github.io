Guidance, Navigation and Control. Personal project.

Steering a baseball into the strike zone with spin alone

A closed-loop 6-DOF GNC stack for a pitched baseball. The ball carries one IMU and three reaction wheels, and its only steering force is the Magnus force from its own spin. Over 0.45 s of flight it estimates its state, solves for the spin that puts it on the target, and torques itself onto that spin. Strike rate goes from 23 % to 100 %.

Summary

What the project is, how it is built, and what it does not claim.

Work in progress· numbers below come from the current build

Aerodynamics

A 90 mph fastball reaches the plate in under half a second. In that time the Magnus force moves it more than a foot off the path gravity alone would give. A spinning ball deflects its own wake and is pushed perpendicular to both its spin axis and its velocity. Backspin lifts, topspin drops, sidespin breaks, and spin pointed along the velocity vector does nothing.

That makes spin a usable control input with three hard limits. The force is always perpendicular to velocity, its magnitude saturates with spin rate, and its direction only changes as fast as the ball's angular momentum can be reoriented.

The strike zone

The target is a 0.432 m by 0.55 m box at a plane 16.8 m from release. The ball either crosses inside it or it does not, which gives a clean terminal miss distance and a pass/fail metric. Guidance aims at the centre of the zone.

Building the stack end to end

The point of the project was to write every layer of a GNC system myself rather than study them separately. Each layer feeds the one above it.

  • Plant. 6-DOF quaternion rigid-body dynamics with reaction wheels, integrated with DOP853.
  • Environment. Drag plus a spin-dependent Magnus model, validated against published pitch movement.
  • Sensors. A 6-DOF IMU with white noise and a bias drawn once per flight.
  • Navigation. A 22-state EKF estimating state, wheel speeds and IMU biases.
  • Guidance. A closed-form solve that maps terminal position error to a commanded inertial spin vector.
  • Control. A feedback-linearising reaction-wheel loop that cancels gyroscopic coupling and slews the ball onto that spin.
  • Verification. A seeded Monte Carlo over pitcher release dispersion.

Guidance never sees truth. It runs on the EKF estimate, and the EKF evaluates its onboard aerodynamic model at its own estimate.

Headline result

200 pitches with release dispersion in angle, speed, spin rate and release point. Each pitch is flown twice, once torque-free as a baseline and once under the full stack.

Scope

The reaction wheels are not sized to a real motor. A 5 N·m flywheel does not fit inside a baseball. That one component is idealised so the question under test is whether the estimator and the guidance law work. Mass properties, air density, drag and lift coefficients, sensor noise and bias, filter tuning and integration are all physical.

01

Dynamics and Physics

The plant, the aerodynamics, the IMU and the actuators.

Motivation for choices

The control input is spin, so attitude has to be a state. A point-mass model would hide the gyroscopic dynamics and would let guidance command spin no actuator could produce. Attitude is stored as a quaternion to avoid gimbal lock and to make rotating an inertial spin command into body axes cheap.

The ball is in free flight, so the actuator has to be internal. Three orthogonal reaction wheels give three-axis authority and reproduce the momentum coupling a real system would have.

$C_D$ is modelled as constant because it is roughly flat over the Reynolds range of a pitch. $C_L$ is not, so it is a function of spin. Its saturation sets the control authority limit.

Methodology

State vector

13 physical states plus one speed per wheel $$ X = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{r}_i^{\mathsf T} & \mathbf{v}_i^{\mathsf T} & \mathbf{q}_{ib}^{\mathsf T} & \boldsymbol\omega_b^{\mathsf T} & \boldsymbol\Omega^{\mathsf T} \end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf T} \in \mathbb{R}^{13+n} $$

Position and velocity are inertial, $\mathbf q_{ib}$ rotates body to inertial, $\boldsymbol\omega_b$ is the body rate and $\boldsymbol\Omega$ holds wheel speeds. The inertial frame sits at the plate with $-y$ the direction of travel and $+z$ up.

Aerodynamics

dynamic pressure, drag, Magnus lift $$ \bar q = \tfrac12 \rho \lVert \mathbf v_i \rVert^2, \qquad \mathbf F_D = -\,\bar q\, C_D A \, \hat{\mathbf v}, \qquad \mathbf F_L = \bar q\, C_L(\omega)\, A \, \frac{\boldsymbol\omega_i \times \hat{\mathbf v}} {\lVert \boldsymbol\omega_i \times \hat{\mathbf v} \rVert} $$

The Magnus force uses the inertial spin $\boldsymbol\omega_i = R(\mathbf q)\,\boldsymbol\omega_b$. The lift coefficient is a saturating empirical fit:

ω in rad/s $$ C_L(\omega) = 0.319\left(1 - e^{-2.48\times10^{-3}\,\omega}\right) $$

Two properties of this curve matter later. It is invertible, so a required $C_L$ maps to a unique spin rate, and it saturates at $C_{L,\max}=0.319$, which caps how hard the ball can turn.

Aerodynamic spin-down torque is set to zero. Spin decay over a 0.45 s flight is small enough to ignore.

Rigid-body dynamics with wheels

Wheel axes are stacked in $A \in \mathbb{R}^{3\times n}$ with inertias $I_w$.

angular momentum, attitude dynamics, translation $$ \mathbf L_b = J\,\boldsymbol\omega_b + A\,(I_w \odot \boldsymbol\Omega), \qquad \dot{\boldsymbol\omega}_b = J_{\text{eff}}^{-1} \left( \mathbf T_{\text{ext}} - A\,\boldsymbol\tau_w - \boldsymbol\omega_b \times \mathbf L_b \right) $$ $$ \dot{\boldsymbol\Omega} = \boldsymbol\tau_w \oslash I_w - A^{\mathsf T} \dot{\boldsymbol\omega}_b, \qquad \dot{\mathbf q} = \tfrac12\,\Xi(\mathbf q)\,\boldsymbol\omega_b, \qquad \dot{\mathbf v}_i = \mathbf g_i + \frac{\mathbf F_{\text{aero}}}{m} $$

The $-A\boldsymbol\tau_w$ term is the reaction: torque applied to a wheel appears on the ball with the opposite sign.

Sensors

Three-axis gyro and three-axis accelerometer, each with white noise and a constant bias drawn once per flight. The accelerometer senses specific force only, so gravity does not appear in the measurement.

IMU measurement model $$ \mathbf z_g = \boldsymbol\omega_b + \mathbf b_g + \boldsymbol\eta_g, \qquad \mathbf z_a = R(\mathbf q)^{\mathsf T} \frac{\mathbf F_{\text{aero}}}{m} + \mathbf b_a + \boldsymbol\eta_a $$

The accelerometer therefore only sees the aerodynamic force, which depends on velocity, attitude and spin but not on position.

Actuators

Three orthogonal wheels, $I_w = 3\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm{kg\,m^2}$ each, torque saturated at $\pm 5\ \mathrm{N\,m}$. Saturation is applied inside the dynamics, not only in the controller.

02

Navigation

Estimating position, velocity, attitude and spin from an IMU only.

Motivation for choices

A MEMS IMU is the only sensor that plausibly fits inside a baseball. GPS cannot get a fix in 0.45 s and a camera needs compute the ball does not have.

Because the accelerometer only senses the aerodynamic force, position is not observable. It is dead reckoned and its error is bounded by the velocity estimate. The filter carries it anyway and the Monte Carlo reports the resulting drift.

Two extra states beyond a standard INS filter. Wheel speeds, because wheel momentum enters the ball's rotational dynamics through $\mathbf L_b$ and the commanded torque makes them nearly free to track. IMU biases, because an unmodelled accel bias is absorbed into attitude and velocity, and velocity error integrates straight into position error.

Methodology

Augmented state

22 elements for n = 3 wheels $$ \hat{\mathbf x} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf r & \mathbf v & \mathbf q & \boldsymbol\omega_b & \boldsymbol\Omega & \mathbf b_g & \mathbf b_a \end{bmatrix}^{\mathsf T} \in \mathbb{R}^{13+n+6} $$

Predict

The prediction integrates the same nonlinear dynamics as the plant, evaluated at the filter estimate and driven by the commanded torque. Biases are modelled as constants.

RK45 propagation, discrete covariance $$ \hat{\mathbf x}^- = \hat{\mathbf x} + \int_{t_0}^{t_1} f\!\left(\hat{\mathbf x}, \boldsymbol\tau_w\right) dt, \qquad A = \left.\frac{\partial f}{\partial \mathbf x}\right|_{\hat{\mathbf x}}, \quad A_d = I + A\,\Delta t $$ $$ P^- = A_d\, P\, A_d^{\mathsf T} + Q\,\Delta t $$

$Q$ is scaled by $\Delta t$ because it is a continuous-time noise density. Adding it whole each step would make the tuning depend on the rate the filter happens to run at.

Update

The predicted measurement includes the modelled aerodynamic force. The covariance update uses Joseph form so it stays symmetric positive definite.

Joseph form $$ \mathbf y = \mathbf z - h(\hat{\mathbf x}^-), \qquad S = H P^- H^{\mathsf T} + R, \qquad K = P^- H^{\mathsf T} S^{-1} $$ $$ \hat{\mathbf x}^+ = \hat{\mathbf x}^- + K\mathbf y, \qquad P^+ = (I - KH)\,P^-\,(I - KH)^{\mathsf T} + K R K^{\mathsf T} $$

Both $A$ and $H$ are numerical forward differences with the quaternion renormalised at each perturbation. That is fast enough at 500 Hz and cannot drift out of sync with the dynamics.

Initialisation

The filter starts from a perturbed estimate, not truth. Velocity and attitude are seeded with release uncertainty and the biases with the residual left after a pre-launch multi-position calibration, which rolls the ball through known orientations so gravity moves in body axes while bias does not.

tilt error from residual bias; small-angle quaternion is half the angle $$ \sigma_{\text{tilt}} = \arcsin\!\left(\frac{\lVert \mathbf b_{\text{horiz}} \rVert}{g}\right), \qquad \sigma_q = \tfrac12 \sigma_{\text{tilt}} $$

03

Guidance

Mapping a terminal position error to a commanded inertial spin vector.

Motivation for choices

Guidance takes a position error at a plane 16.8 m away and has to return a body-rate command. The map is constrained: Magnus force is always perpendicular to velocity, its magnitude saturates at $C_{L,\max}$, its direction is set by a spin axis the inner loop can only slew at a finite rate, and the horizon is 0.45 s and shrinking.

The law inverts the aerodynamics in closed form instead of searching for a solution. It asks what constant acceleration would put the ball on the target in the time remaining, removes the parts it does not control, projects out the part Magnus cannot produce, and inverts the $C_L$ curve for the spin rate. No ODE solves, no Jacobians, no iteration, and every intermediate quantity can be plotted and checked.

Methodology

Each outer tick, from the estimated state $(\hat{\mathbf r}, \hat{\mathbf v}, \hat{\mathbf q})$:

1. Time to go

Along-track speed is nearly constant, so time to go comes from the geometry.

$$ t_{\text{go}} = \frac{y_{\text{target}} - \hat y}{\hat v_y} $$

2. Required constant acceleration

Solve the two-point boundary value problem under constant acceleration.

$$ \mathbf a_{\text{req}} = \frac{2\left(\mathbf r_{\text{target}} - \hat{\mathbf r} - \hat{\mathbf v}\,t_{\text{go}}\right)}{t_{\text{go}}^2} $$

3. Remove what is not commanded

Gravity and drag act regardless of the controller, so subtract them.

$$ \mathbf a_{\text{cmd}} = \mathbf a_{\text{req}} - \mathbf g - \underbrace{\left(-\frac{\bar q\, C_D A}{m}\,\hat{\mathbf v}\right)}_{\text{drag}} $$

4. Project onto what Magnus can produce

The along-track component is unachievable, so project it out.

$$ \mathbf a_\perp = \mathbf a_{\text{cmd}} - \hat{\mathbf v}\,\frac{\hat{\mathbf v}\cdot\mathbf a_{\text{cmd}}}{\lVert \hat{\mathbf v}\rVert^2} $$

The gap between $\mathbf a_{\text{cmd}}$ and $\mathbf a_\perp$ is the part of the command the physics cannot deliver, and it is directly plottable.

5. Invert the aerodynamics

Set the Magnus force equal to $m\,\mathbf a_\perp$ and solve for the lift coefficient, clipped just short of saturation so the inverse stays finite.

required lift coefficient, then invert C_L(ω) $$ C_{L,\text{req}} = \min\!\left(\frac{m \lVert \mathbf a_\perp \rVert}{\bar q\,A},\; 0.99\,C_{L,\max}\right) $$ $$ \lVert\boldsymbol\omega_{\text{cmd}}\rVert = \frac{-\ln\!\left(1 - C_{L,\text{req}}/C_{L,\max}\right)}{2.48\times10^{-3}} $$

The direction comes from the Magnus cross product.

$$ \hat{\boldsymbol\omega}_{\text{cmd}} = \frac{\hat{\mathbf v}\times\mathbf a_\perp}{\lVert \hat{\mathbf v}\times\mathbf a_\perp \rVert} $$

Check: with $\boldsymbol\omega \propto \mathbf v \times \mathbf a_\perp$ and $\mathbf a_\perp \perp \mathbf v$, the triple product expands to

$$ \left(\mathbf v\times\mathbf a_\perp\right)\times\mathbf v = \lVert\mathbf v\rVert^2\,\mathbf a_\perp $$

so the lift points where it was asked to.

6. Rotate into body axes

$$ \boldsymbol\omega_{\text{cmd}}^{b} = R(\hat{\mathbf q})^{\mathsf T}\,\boldsymbol\omega_{\text{cmd}}^{i} $$
Implementation

The law is w_setpoint() in gnc_kinematic.py, about thirty lines with no solver and no internal state. It runs at 50 Hz and the inner loop holds the last command in between. 

# gnc_kinematic.py, the guidance law in full
t_go   = (target[1] - r_i[1]) / v_i[1]
a      = 2 * (target - r_i - v_i * t_go) / (t_go**2)     # required accel
a_cmd  = a - a_g - a_d                                  # strip gravity + drag
a_perp = a_cmd - v_i * np.dot(v_i, a_cmd) / v_norm2     # strip along-track

C_L_req  = min(np.linalg.norm(a_perp) * p.m / (q_dyn * p.area), C_L_max*.99)
w_cmd_i  = np.cross(v_i, a_perp) / v_norm2              # direction
w_cmd_i /= np.linalg.norm(w_cmd_i)
w_cmd_i *= -np.log(1 - C_L_req/C_L_max) / 2.48E-3       # invert C_L(w)

Known limitations

  • The $1/t_{\text{go}}^2$ singularity. Commanded acceleration blows up near the plate, so the commanded spin pins to saturation in the last few hundredths of a second. It costs nothing because there is no time left to respond, but a terminal freeze would remove it.
  • Constant acceleration is an approximation. Re-solving at 50 Hz means it only has to hold for 20 ms at a time.
  • The axes are not equivalent. Vertical correction modulates existing backspin. Lateral correction has to reorient the spin axis, which costs more.
04

Control

Slewing the body rate onto the commanded spin vector.

Motivation for choices

The ball is already spinning at roughly 230 rad/s, so the gyroscopic term $\boldsymbol\omega \times \mathbf L$ is large and resists any change of axis. The wheels add to $\mathbf L$ themselves, and the command updates at 50 Hz inside a 0.45 s flight.

The gyroscopic term is known exactly from states the EKF already estimates, so the loop cancels it instead of rejecting it as a disturbance. That leaves one tuning parameter with physical meaning, a settling time, rather than a gain that changes with spin rate or wheel layout.

Positive torque on a wheel spins the ball the other way. The loop numerically probes $\partial \dot{\boldsymbol\omega}/\partial\boldsymbol\tau$ once at construction and builds its gain from that, so the sign cannot be wrong.

Methodology

Cancel the gyroscopic coupling by projecting the known cross term onto the wheel axes.

$$ \boldsymbol\tau_{\text{gyro}} = -A^{\mathsf T}\left(\boldsymbol\omega_b \times \mathbf L_b\right), \qquad \mathbf L_b = J\boldsymbol\omega_b + A(I_w \odot \boldsymbol\Omega) $$

Then drive the rate error to zero on a chosen time constant.

first-order rate loop, t_settle = 0.02 s $$ \dot{\boldsymbol\omega}_{\text{des}} = \frac{\boldsymbol\omega_{\text{cmd}}^{b} - \boldsymbol\omega_b}{t_{\text{settle}}}, \qquad \boldsymbol\tau_{\text{fb}} = -A^{\mathsf T}\left(J_{\text{eff}}\,\dot{\boldsymbol\omega}_{\text{des}}\right) $$ $$ \boldsymbol\tau = \operatorname{clip}\!\left(\boldsymbol\tau_{\text{gyro}} + \boldsymbol\tau_{\text{fb}},\; \pm\tau_{\max}\right) $$

Substituting into the Euler equation cancels $\boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf L$ and leaves first-order error dynamics $\dot{\mathbf e} = -\mathbf e / t_{\text{settle}}$. At 20 ms the inner loop settles in about 2 % of the flight.

Rates

Inner loop and EKF at 500 Hz, guidance at 50 Hz. The 10:1 separation lets guidance treat the inner loop as instantaneous. Torque is zero-order-hold between control ticks, so the plant sees a piecewise constant input.

Implementation
gnc_kinematic.pyw_setpoint() and inner_loop(), plus the driver that runs navigation, guidance and control at their rates and returns a single tau(t, x) callable.
simulate.pysimulate_gnc(), the zero-order-hold driver. The GNC law must not read truth unless it is deliberately modelling perfect navigation.

The whole stack is exposed to the simulator as one closure tau(t, x_true). Everything inside it, estimator included, is private to the law.

05

Monte Carlo

200 dispersed pitches, each flown twice.

Motivation for choices

Dispersion is drawn from release conditions rather than abstract disturbances, because that is the variation a real pitcher cannot remove. Release position offset is the hardest case: a release angle error is visible early and guidance has the whole flight to work against it, while a position offset is a pure bias that has to be flown out.

Vertical and lateral miss are reported separately. Vertical correction modulates existing backspin, lateral correction reorients the spin axis, and averaging them hides which one is limiting.

Each trial seeds its dispersion from RandomState(4000 + i) and its sensor noise from seed i, so any single pitch can be re-run and reproduced exactly.

Methodology

Dispersion, 1σ:

  • Release angle 0.35° in yaw and pitch.
  • Speed 1.5 mph, spin rate 100 rpm.
  • Release point 0.15 m in both $x$ and $z$.

Per trial:

  1. Draw the dispersion from the trial's seeded generator.
  2. Fly the baseline: torque-free, DOP853, tight tolerances.
  3. Fly the controlled pitch: fresh IMU biases, fresh EKF from a perturbed estimate, guidance at 50 Hz, control and filter at 500 Hz.
  4. Terminate at the plate or the ground and record the crossing.

The p90 matters more than the median. A controller that fixes easy pitches and gives up on hard ones has a good median and a bad tail. Here p90 miss falls from 0.660 m to 0.094 m.

What the Monte Carlo shows

  • Terminal saturation is real but harmless. Peak torque hits the limit in most trials, only in the last few hundredths of a second. Freezing guidance in the terminal phase would remove it.
  • The spin command is not tracked at the end. By then there is no flight time left for spin to produce displacement, so the metric is misleading and a time-weighted version would be better.
  • Navigation is no longer the limit. EKF position error ends near 0.014 m and the miss ends near 0.014 m. Further gains have to come from guidance.

07

Next steps

Terminal guidance freeze

Stop re-solving inside the last 10 % of flight. Removes the $1/t_{\text{go}}^2$ spike and the terminal torque chatter.

Time-weighted tracking metric

Weight spin tracking error by remaining flight time so it measures something the ball can act on.

Actuator sizing study

Sweep torque limit and wheel inertia to find the smallest actuator that still holds the strike rate.

Seam-dependent lift

The current $C_L$ is orientation-independent. A seam-phase model would close the gap on the validation table.

Wind and atmosphere

Wiring a wind vector through the state-based aero path adds a disturbance the EKF has to absorb.

Colourblind-safe figures

Move the matplotlib plots to an Okabe-Ito palette with dash-pattern redundancy, as the guidance panels already use.
